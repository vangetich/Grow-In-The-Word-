# Learner Identity, Progressive Autonomy & Care Pathway

**Status:** Product and architecture proposal informed by owner feedback; legal and safeguarding review required before implementation.

## 1. Product principles
Grow In The Word is an explicitly Christian learning and spiritual formation product. It must not assume every learner lives with two Christian parents, has a conventional family structure, or has access to a parent who can operate an account. Children must be treated with dignity regardless of family structure, religion or lack of religious affiliation.

Separate three concepts:
- **Legal authority:** who may authorize processing and make decisions under applicable law.
- **Account autonomy:** what actions the learner can perform within the product.
- **Spiritual development:** educational and faith formation experiences. It is not a legal-capacity test, access-control score or measure of spiritual worth.

## 2. Proposed age experiences
| Product band | Experience | Default account pattern |
|---|---|---|
| 3–7 | Bedtime Shepherd | Parent/authorized guardian-managed learner profile |
| 8–13 | Guided Explorer | Managed profile with age-appropriate agency |
| 14–17 | Emerging Independence | Personal login/space where lawful, with scoped and transparent guardian support |
| 18+ | Independent Adult | Adult-controlled account and permissions |

These are product experience bands, not legal conclusions. The under-18 status and applicable consent rules must be evaluated per jurisdiction. In Kenya, the Children Act defines a child as a person under 18; section 33 of the Data Protection Act addresses parental/guardian consent and age-verification mechanisms for processing children's personal data. Obtain qualified local legal advice before finalizing onboarding or account autonomy.

## 3. Progressive autonomy
From the Emerging Independence band, the product may offer a separate login, personal dashboard, permitted preference management and independent learning activities. Guardian permissions must be granular, transparent to the learner and based on verified legal authority, consent and applicable law. A guardian's access must not expand silently. Private reflection remains excluded from family projections by default.

At 18, ordinarily transition the account to adult control and revoke prior guardian access unless the now-adult learner explicitly authorizes continued access or another lawful arrangement applies. Any exception requires legal review. Spiritual maturity may inform learning pathways, mentoring and responsibility-building content, but must not override legal rights or determine technical permissions.

## 4. Care & Access Pathway
Support distinct pathways:
1. Parent with parental responsibility.
2. Legally appointed guardian.
3. Verified kinship/foster/alternative caregiver with appropriate authority.
4. Authorized institutional care representative acting within a documented legal/organizational mandate.
5. No verified caregiver or uncertain authority.

A relative, caregiver, church leader, teacher, mentor or institution employee must not receive guardian-level access solely by self-declaration or job title. Verify authority proportionately and minimize document collection. Define review, expiry, revocation, dispute and safeguarding escalation.

For a child without a verified responsible adult, consider access to public, non-personalized learning resources without creating a personal learner record or collecting sensitive information. Do not offer personalized tracking, private journal storage, family features or AI processing of personal data until the required legal basis, consent/authority and safeguards are established. Do not ask a child to upload sensitive legal documents as a workaround.

Institutional access must be scoped to approved learners and tasks. An institution-level account must not automatically expose all private learner content. Avoid bulk access to journal content and private reflections.

## 5. Non-Christian and religiously diverse households
Be transparent that the product teaches from a Christian biblical perspective. Do not misrepresent its purpose. Provide clear parent/guardian information about content and data processing, and allow informed participation decisions. Do not discriminate against a child because their household is non-Christian or non-religious. Do not infer a child's religion from browsing or profile behavior. Religious reflection and belief-related data should be treated as sensitive and purpose-limited. No coercive prompts, disclosure to family members by default, or use of child data for religious pressure.

Access to public general learning content should not require a declaration of Christian identity. Where personal data is processed for a minor, apply the relevant parental/guardian consent and child-best-interests requirements.

## 6. Policy and data requirements
The identity model must distinguish account subject, learner profile, legal representative, care relationship, authority evidence, consent record, autonomy policy and resource permissions. Authority is time-bounded and revocable. Keep a provenance trail of how authority was established without retaining unnecessary identity documents.

## 7. Required legal/safeguarding review
Review Kenya's Children Act 2022, Data Protection Act 2019, ODPC 2025 Guidance Notes for Processing Children's Data, applicable regulations, and laws of each future launch jurisdiction. Obtain advice on institutional authority, foster/kinship care, age assurance, religious/belief data, child assent, independent accounts, retention and exceptions. This document is product architecture guidance, not legal advice.
