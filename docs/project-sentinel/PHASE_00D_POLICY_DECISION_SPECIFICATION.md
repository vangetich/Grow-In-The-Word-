# Project Sentinel — Phase 00D Policy Decision Specification

**Product:** Grow In The Word  
**Phase:** 00D — International Governance  
**Status:** Draft conceptual specification; owner review and jurisdiction counsel review required  
**Depends on:** Phase 00C identity, authorization, consent, data-flow and service-contract architecture; Phase 00D Jurisdiction Rule Catalogue  
**Implementation status:** Not implemented. This document does not authorize a policy engine, schema, API, deployment or real-data processing.

---

## 1. Objective

Define how a future policy decision service should evaluate a requested product action against approved jurisdiction rules and return a traceable decision. The service must be deterministic for the same approved policy version and equivalent inputs, explain its outcome, and fail safely when material facts or legal interpretations are unknown.

The service is a policy enforcement component, not a legal reasoning model. It must evaluate only approved, versioned rules. It must not invent law, infer legal authority from social relationships, or use an LLM to decide legal capacity, safeguarding credibility or sensitive-data legality.

## 2. Architecture boundary

The future policy decision service sits behind the Phase 00C service boundary and consumes trusted, purpose-limited facts from identity, consent, authority, data classification, provider and content-rights services.

It does not:
- authenticate users;
- decide who is a parent or guardian from free text;
- create consent or legal authority;
- determine a person's age from an image or AI guess;
- decide the meaning of ambiguous legislation;
- replace a DPIA, legal review or safeguarding investigation;
- decide spiritual maturity or theological standing;
- create a physical database schema;
- directly call AI, analytics, email or storage providers.

The calling service remains responsible for enforcing the returned decision. A UI-only decision is insufficient.

## 3. Decision request contract (conceptual)

A future request should contain, at minimum:

| Input | Meaning | Trust requirement |
|---|---|---|
| request_id | Unique trace identifier | Generated by trusted service |
| actor_id | Authenticated actor requesting action | Identity service verified |
| subject_learner_id | Learner/resource affected | Resolved server-side; never trust client-supplied ownership |
| action | Specific requested operation | Enumerated operation, not arbitrary text |
| purpose_id | Discrete processing purpose | Approved purpose registry |
| jurisdiction_context | Subject location, targeted market, establishment and relevant processing territory | Evidence-backed, dated and uncertainty-aware |
| age_assessment | Age band/threshold result, method, date and confidence state | Proportionate evidence; do not retain raw ID by default |
| data_classification | Data categories involved | Trusted classifier or explicit service metadata; unknown is not ordinary |
| authority_context | Legal role, evidence status, scope, expiry and revocation state | Authority service; identity alone is insufficient |
| consent_context | Purpose-specific notice/consent/assent records and validity | Consent service; no blanket consent |
| provider_context | Controller/processor/subprocessor and all relevant access/storage locations | Approved vendor inventory |
| rights_context | Content item, licence, territory, mode, term and permitted uses | Rights inventory |
| policy_version | Exact approved rule catalogue version | Server-controlled |
| request_time | UTC timestamp | Trusted server clock |

Missing or stale facts must be explicitly represented as UNKNOWN, NOT_VERIFIED, EXPIRED or NOT_APPLICABLE. They must never be silently defaulted to permissive values.

## 4. Evaluation pipeline

The evaluator MUST execute these stages in order:

### Stage A — Request integrity and actor authentication
- Confirm the actor is authenticated and allowed to request this class of operation.
- Resolve learner/resource ownership server-side.
- Reject malformed or unrecognized actions.
- Do not treat a client-provided learner ID or role label as proof of authority.

### Stage B — Jurisdiction resolution
- Determine relevant jurisdictions from the subject's location, service targeting, controller establishment, processing location, recipient and subject matter.
- Resolve Tanzania Mainland versus Zanzibar and whether the relevant matter is Union or non-Union.
- If material jurisdiction facts are unknown or conflicting, return HOLD_FOR_REVIEW.
- Do not select a jurisdiction based solely on device language, IP address or a user-editable country preference.

### Stage C — Rule/source validity
- Load only rules with approved status for the exact policy version.
- Confirm source version, effective dates, territorial scope and status.
- If a required rule is OPEN, suspended, expired or source validity is unresolved, return HOLD_FOR_REVIEW.
- Do not use draft or counsel-pending interpretations as production authority.

### Stage D — Subject and age classification
- Evaluate child-protection status separately from each jurisdiction's data-consent threshold.
- Preserve the Rwanda distinction between under-16 Article 9 consent trigger and under-18 child-protection safeguards.
- Preserve product under-18 safeguarding baseline as BASELINE, not as a universal statutory consent age.
- If age/knowledge status is material but unknown, return HOLD_FOR_REVIEW.

### Stage E — Data classification
- Identify all data categories used, generated, inferred, transmitted or exposed by the action.
- Religious belief/reflection data, child data, journal content, voice and other sensitive categories must not be downgraded because they are encrypted, pseudonymous or processed by a vendor.
- If classification is unknown, return HOLD_FOR_REVIEW where a sensitive category is plausible and material.

### Stage F — Purpose and legal basis
- Evaluate each purpose independently.
- Verify purpose-specific lawful basis, notice, consent or other authorization where required.
- Consent for profile creation must not authorize AI, analytics, voice, sharing, sponsor reporting or model training.
- A purpose change requires a new policy evaluation.

### Stage G — Authority and child participation
- Verify the legal authority route, evidence, scope, expiry, disputes and revocation.
- Evaluate child assent/participation separately where appropriate.
- Do not grant access to a caregiver, institution, church, mentor or sponsor solely from title, relationship, employment or funding.
- Do not disclose private journal content by default.

### Stage H — Safeguards and risk controls
- Require best-interests assessment, DPIA, minimization, retention, security, rights handling and operational safeguards where applicable.
- A high residual risk or missing mandatory assessment produces HOLD_FOR_REVIEW or DENY as the approved rule specifies.
- A safeguard is effective only if it is enforceable and evidenced, not merely displayed in UI copy.

### Stage I — Provider, transfer and storage
- Evaluate every processor, subprocessor, remote support location, backup, telemetry route and AI provider involved.
- Apply destination-specific transfer requirements.
- Treat Rwanda Article 50 foreign-storage authorization separately from Articles 48–49 transfer analysis.
- Do not infer that an approved transfer automatically authorizes foreign storage or vice versa.
- Unknown provider locations or missing required contracts produce HOLD_FOR_REVIEW.

### Stage J — Content rights
- If the action distributes, displays, transforms, caches, translates, streams, stores, makes available or sends content to AI, evaluate the exact rights grant for that action.
- Rights for one edition, medium, territory or purpose do not imply rights for another.
- Missing licence scope or expired rights produce HOLD_FOR_REVIEW; known prohibition produces DENY.

### Stage K — Decision and enforcement
- Combine applicable rule results using the precedence rules in the Rule Catalogue.
- Return one primary outcome, mandatory controls, reason codes, rule IDs, evidence references, policy version and review metadata.
- The calling service must enforce the outcome server-side before the action occurs.
- Log the minimum decision evidence needed for accountability; do not log journal text, prayer content, identity documents or unnecessary sensitive data.

## 5. Decision combination semantics

Use this order of precedence:

1. Any applicable DENY rule results in DENY.
2. Otherwise, any applicable HOLD_FOR_REVIEW rule results in HOLD_FOR_REVIEW.
3. Otherwise, if required controls are satisfied and enforceable, return ALLOW_WITH_CONTROLS.
4. Otherwise, return ALLOW only when all applicable rules are satisfied and no additional controls are required.
5. NOT_APPLICABLE is recorded per rule; it is not a standalone permission to perform the action.

A missing input is not a negative predicate. UNKNOWN must not cause a protective rule to be skipped.

## 6. Decision response contract (conceptual)

| Output | Meaning |
|---|---|
| decision_id | Unique audit trace ID |
| outcome | ALLOW / ALLOW_WITH_CONTROLS / DENY / HOLD_FOR_REVIEW |
| reason_codes | Stable, non-sensitive explanation codes |
| applicable_rule_ids | Rules evaluated and applied |
| not_applicable_rule_ids | Rules evaluated and found not applicable, with rationale |
| required_controls | Enforceable controls required before or during action |
| missing_evidence | Evidence types still required; never expose unnecessary sensitive details |
| policy_version | Immutable version evaluated |
| jurisdiction_resolution | Resolved jurisdictions and confidence/unknown state |
| evaluated_at | Server timestamp |
| review_route | Owner/legal/privacy/safeguarding queue when held |
| audit_reference | Reference to minimized decision record |

The response must not include legal advice generated by AI or reveal private data to an unauthorized actor.

## 8. Feature decision profiles

The following profiles define the minimum independent purpose gates. They are not implementation permission.

| Feature/action | Separate purposes to evaluate | Default disposition until approved |
|---|---|---|
| Public Bible reading | Content display, analytics, preferences | Public reading may be considered separately from personal tracking; content rights still required |
| Learner profile | Account, progress record, personalization | HOLD until identity, child authorization and privacy basis are approved |
| Growth analytics | Measurement, profiling, recommendations | No sensitive-belief inference; HOLD until DPIA/purpose and age controls approved |
| Prayer/reflection journal | Capture, storage, retrieval, export, deletion | Excluded from AI/analytics/family/sponsor access by default |
| AI tutor | Prompt content, provider disclosure, retention, evaluation/training | No child or sensitive content until provider and purpose gates approved |
| Voice practice | Recording, transcription, speech assessment, retention | HOLD until voice/child/DPIA/provider/consent review complete |
| Parent dashboard | Learner progress and account administration | Scope-limited; private reflections excluded by default |
| Institution/sponsor | Funding, assigned learner administration, aggregate reporting | No parental authority or private-data access by funding/role alone |
| Community/sharing | Public posting, moderation, messaging, reporting | Disabled for children until safeguarding, moderation and legal review |
| Offline access | Local storage, sync, device loss, deletion, rights | Only content with offline rights and tested security/sync behavior |
| Content/audio/video | Display, reproduction, stream, cache, download, adaptation | Item-level rights must authorize exact use |
| Safeguarding report | Report intake, triage, referral, evidence retention | Requires protected access and locally reviewed response procedure |

## 9. Prohibited decision inputs and outputs

The service MUST NOT use:
- spiritual maturity, holiness, salvation, divine favour or perceived faithfulness;
- Bible knowledge or devotional frequency;
- engagement streaks, XP, level, rank or monetization status;
- inferred religion, ethnicity or family status;
- AI-generated credibility scores for abuse reports;
- a caregiver's job title, church role or self-declared relationship as proof of legal authority.

The service MUST NOT output:
- spiritual-worth scores;
- legal-capacity conclusions from developmental/spiritual progress;
- automated credibility or guilt findings about safeguarding reports;
- unrestricted parent/institution/sponsor access to private reflections;
- a blanket “compliant” or “approved country” flag without scoped evidence.

## 10. Fail-safe and exception handling

- If a legal source is uncertain, HOLD.
- If jurisdiction is uncertain, HOLD.
- If age is uncertain and material, HOLD.
- If authority is disputed, expired or unverified, suspend affected permissions and HOLD.
- If a required transfer/storage condition is missing, block that data flow.
- If a content licence is missing or ambiguous, block that content/use.
- If the policy service is unavailable, sensitive or child-related actions fail closed; only explicitly approved low-risk public content may remain available.
- Exceptions require a named approver, reason, scope, expiry, compensating controls, counsel reference where law is implicated and audit trail. No permanent silent override.

## 11. Versioning, explainability and audit

- Each release has immutable policy version, effective date, author, reviewer and change summary.
- Every decision stores rule IDs and version, not a copied legal essay.
- Re-evaluate when jurisdiction, age, purpose, data class, authority, consent, provider, location, licence or policy version changes.
- Preserve decision records only for an approved retention period.
- Audit logs must not contain raw journal content, prayer content, access tokens or full identity documents.
- Provide a safe explanation to the user without exposing protected information or disclosing internal safeguarding workflows.

## 12. Phase 00D exit gates for this specification

Before any implementation may be proposed:

1. Owner reviews and approves the conceptual input/output contract.
2. Each LAW/REG/IP rule has current authoritative source and status verified.
3. Local counsel reviews child-data, sensitive-belief, authority, transfer/storage, online-safety and IP rules for each market.
4. Tanzania Zanzibar has a separate assessment and is not implicitly covered by Mainland analysis.
5. Product, privacy, safeguarding, security, content and engineering owners are assigned.
6. Policy tests cover both allowed and denied/held cases, including missing and conflicting inputs.
7. Phase 00C service boundaries are reconciled; no unauthorized schema or provider decision is made.
8. Security review confirms server-side enforcement and least-privilege audit behavior.
9. Owner explicitly authorizes a later implementation phase.

## 13. Disposition

**Draft conceptual specification only.** It is suitable for structured review and test design. It is not an executable specification, legal opinion, implementation approval, compliance certificate or launch authorization.
